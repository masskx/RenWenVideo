=== COMPUTE GROWTH ===

Here's a plot of compute used to train notable AI models starting at the very beginning of the Anthropocene, around around 1950. You can see by the way a change in slope here where AI models transitioned from being like academically interesting to being commercially relevant. Each tick on the y-axis here is two orders of magnitude larger than the one below it. The compute used to train the biggest models has increased about 10 million fold in the last decade. In the upper right I've circled a very rough plausible range for the compute performed by [the] human brain in an entire lifetime. My own like back-of-the-envelope estimate: if you imagine that every synapse does like one flop per millisecond then you get about one trillion petaflops of like compute in the human brain over a lifetime. And that would put lifetime human compute like a fraction of a grid division above the biggest model on this chart. So we are getting to scales of model training compute similar to those in a human brain across the lifetime. This is like not sufficient and probably not even necessary to approach like human levels of intelligence, but it maybe makes the comparison somehow a little bit more plausible.

=== PhD / CAREER ADVICE ===
[25:25–27:12 approx; ASR]
This talk was motivated by a conversation I had with a with a very capable grad student. And they were talking about how they thought we were gonna have AGI in three years, and then a few minutes later they were like, oh yeah, I'm gonna finish my PhD in a couple years and then I'm gonna get a postdoc and then after that I figure I'll apply for faculty jobs. And I was [like]: if you believe in like one part of your brain that this like world-changing technology is coming, you should also believe [it] in other parts of your brain. I don't know what an academic job is gonna look like in like 10 or 20 years. I don't know what any job is gonna look like in 10 or 20 years. But I am like very sure it is gonna look very different than it does today. So you should [not] be planning with an assumption that your current career path is like not a stable one. People often make life decisions balancing risk and reward… If AI is going to cause large scale disruption then there is like a high baseline level of unavoidable risk — the safe option is not actually the safe option. So what you are actually doing is you're just sacrificing potential reward without actually buying like more safety. So… totally separate from AI people are way too risk averse, but especially in this context you should do the interesting thing instead of instead of the safe thing.
