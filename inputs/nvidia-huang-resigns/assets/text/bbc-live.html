Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang quits in wake of affair with suspected Chinese spy, as firm's stocks plunge

Summary

  • Jensen Huang has resigned as CEO of tech giant Nvidia, the company's board says, with chief financial officer Colette Kress appointed interim boss

  • The company's shares plummeted more than 13% as US markets opened shortly after news of Huang's resignation

  • It comes after the New York Times reported that he had a second affair with an executive at his own company

  • On Sunday, Huang admitted an affair with a woman, reportedly from another company - she was also allegedly a Chinese spy

  • Beijing has warned the US not to use the spying allegations as a pretext to once again restrict tech sales to China

  • Nvidia is already investigating the affair Huang has admitted, as is the FBI. What does Nvidia's code of conduct say?

  • Huang steered Nvidia to enormous success, and in an extraordinary and strangely old-fashioned tale, he was ultimately undone by his love life, our technology and AI editor writes

Live Reporting

Edited by Jamie Whitehead and Ben Hatton, with reporting by technology and AI editor Zoe Kleinman and business reporter Rachel Clun in New York

  1. Jensen Huang resigns - how did we get here?published at 15:11 GMT
    15:11 GMT

    29 October: The New York Times first reports Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had a "close relationship" with Alice Huang, who it says US officials allege is a Chinese spy.

    8 November: Jensen Huang admits to having an affair and apologises for being unfaithful to his wife, although he does not say who the other person was. Nvidia’s board of directors begins an independent investigation.

    9 November: US President Donald Trump says the FBI is investigating. Beijing publicly rejects the spying allegations as a smear.

    11 November: The New York Times reports that Jensen Huang had been involved in what it describes as another extramarital relationship, this time with an Nvidia executive who reports to him. Nvidia’s board reportedly holds a meeting to discuss the CEO’s future.

    12 November: Jensen Huang resigns as CEO. Colette Kress, the company’s chief financial officer, is appointed interim CEO.

  3. A giant on the global economic scene whose spell was broken in a weekpublished at 14:55 GMT
    14:55 GMT

    Simon Jack
    Business editor

    It's hard to overstate how giant a figure Jensen Huang has cut on the global economic scene. Insatiable appetite for the chips the company he founded makes, huge investments in other AI related companies, and a personal talismanic brand and profile that has rivalled and possibly surpassed that of even Apple's Steve Jobs in his prime.

    His trademark all-black outfits, and his mischievous charm at product launches gave him rockstar appeal.

    But as an executive at a large financial institution told me, the allegations of an affair with an executive at the Singaporean firm Megaspeed with questions about her links to China, followed by reports of a second affair saw the halo slip and then underlying concerns to emerge.

    Prominent among them was governance - in other words how Nvidia was run. He ruled over an unusually flat management structure with dozens of direct reports to him personally - "a ridiculous set up", according to one executive.

    The promotion of his children to senior posts may work in a family business but are questionable in a $4 trillion publicly traded company.

    Nvidia's role as a cornerstone lender to, and investor in, the rest of the AI industry has raised concerns about the circular financing in the AI industry. It is a fascinating parable to see how quickly the spell he cast over an entire industry was broken in a week.

  6. Huang, who steered Nvidia to enormous success, ultimately undone by his own love lifepublished at 14:20 GMT
    14:20 GMT

    Zoe Kleinman
    Technology and AI editor

    If I’d told you this time last week that Jensen Huang was about to leave Nvidia, you would not have believed me.

    The man who co-founded this enormously successful AI chip company, working tirelessly to build it from the ground up, steering it through some disastrous times with endless energy and ruthless determination, shaping into the giant it has become, ultimately undone by his own love life.

    It’s an extraordinary tale and a strangely old fashioned one for a tech firm with its sites set on the future.

    Colette Kress, who takes over for now, will be a familiar face internally and that must feel reassuring.

    She’s worked at the firm for 13 years and for those who follow Nvidia’s considerable finances, she is often present on earnings calls. She is a billionaire in her own right.

    But she’s not an engineer and will undoubtedly have a very different leadership style to the demanding boss that was Jensen Huang. She will also have to convince investors that its business as usual for Nvidia, and the innovation for which it is renowned will continue at pace.

    A wobbly Nvidia would be bad news for the entire AI industry because its ever-advancing chips drive the most powerful AI models.

    As for Jensen himself - we have to assume the investigation into his relationship with the woman accused of being a Chinese spy will continue, and the results of that may well determine what he does next. He's probably not short of offers at the moment - I expect Nvidia's rivals can't believe their luck.

  7. Who is the new interim Nvidia CEO Colette Kress?published at 14:08 GMT
    14:08 GMT

    Colette Kress, who has just been made the interim CEO of Nvidia, says that she is committed to building on the "the extraordinary foundation" that Jensen Huang has created.

    Kress has been the executive vice president and chief financial officer at Nvidia since joining the company in September 2013.

    The company's board says that she has led the company's finance organisation and "played a key role in Nvidia's growth and evolution".

    Before joining Nvidia, Kress spent nearly 25 years in finance roles at leading technology companies, including Cisco and Microsoft.

    After being appointed as interim chief executive officer, Kress says: "Nvidia's strength has always come from its people, and together with our leadership team, I am committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years.

    "We will continue advancing the work that has made Nvidia a leader in accelerated computing and AI, and execute on the opportunities ahead. Our focus remains on serving our customers, partners, developers and shareholders."

  12. Many people own tech stocks, whether they know it or not, expert sayspublished at 13:41 GMT
    13:41 GMT

    Katie Williams
    Live reporter

    I asked Russ Mould, investment director at AJ Bell, what the latest Nvidia turmoil means for people’s money.

    He says "lots of people probably have this stuff in their pension, whether they know it or not".

    Worth about $4.2tn, Nvidia is one of the "Magnificent Seven" tech stocks, along with Google’s parent company Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Apple, Amazon and Tesla.

    Mould says these seven stocks collectively make up more than 30% of America’s S&P 500, which tracks the share prices of the largest publicly traded US companies.

    And US equities make up a significant portion of many people’s pensions.

    But Matt Britzman, senior equity analyst at Hargreaves Lansdown, tells me that even though Nvidia is an "important" part of the US market, a diversified portfolio is built to "ride out the inevitable ups and downs of individual companies".

    Nvidia’s share price has fallen compared with five days ago, while the Nasdaq Composite, which is heavily weighted towards tech companies, has risen by more than 4%, external.

    Britzman also expects some "short-term turbulence" for Nvidia, but says "demand for AI isn't going anywhere".

  15. What to know about how Nvidia is structuredpublished at 12:37 GMT
    12:37 GMT

    Freya Scott-Turner
    Live reporter

    Nvidia's CEO Jensen Huang is seen as central to the company's success - and this is linked to how the company is structured.

    Let's take a look at how it works.

    The “flat” structure

    In interviews, Huang has spoken about Nvidia's “flat” structure.

    Unlike companies with more typical hierarchies, Huang has no second-in-command. Instead, dozens of people report directly to him.

    The board

    Nvidia's board has 11 members, including Huang. Its powers include hiring and firing the company's chief executive and overseeing company strategy.

    All board members except Huang are considered independent, meaning they have no relationship that the board considers would interfere with independent judgment.

    Regulatory documents also note that all members have an “equal voice” and that there is no permanent board chairperson.

    Huang owns more than 3% of Nvidia's stock

    According to a US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) filing from June, external, Huang owns more than 870 million Nvidia shares, making up just over 3.5% of the company's total stock.

  16. Market analyst remains confident about Nvidia's outlookpublished at 12:11 GMT
    12:11 GMT

    Osmond Chia
    Business reporter, Singapore

    To get a better picture of what happens next, I’ve spoken to market analysis expert Anna Wu.

    Asked whether Jensen Huang would leave Nvidia, Wu says "the board's tone has shifted sharply in a matter of days", from public confidence to weighing up his removal from post.

    "That makes a departure increasingly likely."

    However, she notes that, whatever happens, Huang's influence won’t disappear overnight, as he will remain on the board and be one of the company’s major shareholders.

    On who would replace him, Wu says there’s no obvious successor.

    "The most realistic near-term route is an interim leader from Nvidia's long-serving inner circle, possibly a co-CEO structure pairing a finance and operations veteran such as Colette Kress with a product and technology leader such as Ian Buck."

    Any changes will lead to "near-term turbulence", but Nvidia’s core asset is its chips, and there is no practical substitute at scale in the near term, she adds.

    "Looking through the volatility, I remain confident in Nvidia's outlook."

  18. Who are the board members deciding Jensen Huang's futurepublished at 11:39 GMT
    11:39 GMT

    Philippa Wain
    Technology reporter

    There are 10 Nvidia board members who will decide the fate of CEO Jensen Huang.

    Stephen Neal is Nvidia’s “lead director”, a role similar to that of a chairperson, designated each year. He’s now in the spotlight as the key convener of the board.

    Neal has a legal background, having had a long career at Cooley LLP, one of Silicon Valley’s most influential law firms - perhaps useful experience as discussions of reputational risk take centre stage.

    There are also the long-serving directors. Tench Coxe and Harvey Jones have been at the company for 33 years, since its formation alongside Huang.

    Mark Stevens is also one of Nvidia’s longest-serving directors, having been there at the beginning, though he briefly left for two years and rejoined in 2008.

    These founding-era directors are also by far the biggest shareholders on the board, with vast fortunes tied to the performance of the company and its CEO.

    The majority of directors are men, but four women make up just over a third of the board.

    The longest-serving of them is Dawn Hudson, a former senior leader at Pepsi-Cola.
